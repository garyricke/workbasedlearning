The Legal Pathway

Exploring — and developing — the next generation of legal professionals

Ask a high school student what a lawyer does and you'll hear a courtroom scene from television. The students who know better are almost always the ones whose parents already practice. Field Experience exists to close that gap — and law is the hardest field for us to open, because confidentiality makes attorneys understandably cautious. This page is our answer to that.

What a Placement Can Look Like

  • Observing open court proceedings — public record, zero privilege concern, and the single most clarifying day a student can have.
  • Shadowing a paralegal or legal assistant — often the best entry point, and it shows students the careers that don't require law school.
  • Non-privileged document and file work — organization, indexing, exhibit prep, closing packets, all under supervision.
  • Learning the business of a practice — intake flow, billing, calendaring, client communication, how a firm actually stays open.
  • Intro legal research — public case law and statutes, with a real professional explaining how the reasoning works.
  • Sitting in on closings, depositions, or client meetings — only where you and your client choose to allow it.

On Confidentiality

The concern is legitimate. Here's our answer.
  • Every student signs a written confidentiality agreement before day one — reviewed with the student and their parent or guardian, not just handed over.
  • You draw the line, and we write it down. Many legal placements involve no client file access at all. That is a completely acceptable placement.
  • We know Rule 5.3 puts responsibility on you. Our job is to keep that responsibility small, bounded, and documented — not to hand you a liability.
  • You can end it at any time, for any reason. No explanation owed, no awkwardness. We would rather you stop than stay in something that isn't working.
  • You meet the student before day one. If the fit feels wrong then, we place them elsewhere and nobody is offended.

The Pathway Is Bigger Than "Lawyer"

Attorney Paralegal Legal Assistant Court Reporter Circuit Clerk Title Examiner Compliance Officer Contract Manager Mediator Victim Advocate Probation Officer Legal Operations Judge Court Administrator Investigator Policy Analyst

Most students only know the first one. Half of these are strong, stable careers reachable without law school — and a student can't choose a path they've never heard of.
